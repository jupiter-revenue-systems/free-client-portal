# The Portal That Answers "Where Are We At?"

**You'll have:** a live progress page for one client that shows where things are, what's next, what you need from them and their files, plus a quick way to keep it current.

**Time:** 30 minutes.

**You need:** Claude or ChatGPT, one client project that's running now, and a doc tool that can share a link.

**For the technical:** this is a single source of truth per client. One shared, view-only page with a fixed 5-block layout, rewritten from your raw work log by a prompt. A real portal adds login, per-client data and automatic updates.

## The problem

It's 4 PM. You're deep in the work. An email lands: "Hey, just checking in. Where are we at?"

Nothing is wrong. The project is on time. But the client can't see that, so they ask. You stop, write 3 paragraphs, and lose the thread you were holding.

My clients get a portal. When they want to know where things are, they open it and look. You don't need my portal to get most of that calm. You need one page per client and one habit.

## Part 1. Why clients chase you

A client who asks "where are we at?" isn't being difficult. They paid, and now they can't see anything. Silence feels like nothing is happening.

A progress page only has to answer 4 questions: where are we, what's next, what do you need from me, and where are my files.

**Step 1. Pick one client.** One project that's running now. Don't build this for everyone yet.

**Step 2. Dump your notes.** What's done, what you're on now, what's coming, what you're waiting on from them. Add links to files they should have.

## Part 2. Build the page

**Step 3. Make the first version.** Drag this folder into Claude or ChatGPT and paste this with your notes under it.

```
Build a client progress page from my notes using templates/progress-page.md. Keep the 5 blocks in this order: Where we are (one sentence and the current stage), Done so far, What's next (with a day if I gave one), What I need from you, Your files. Write it to the client, in plain words they'd use, no tool names or jargon unless they already use them. Short lines. Never promise a date I didn't give you. If my notes don't cover a block, ask me one question at a time until they do. Here are my notes:
```

**Step 4. Name your stages.**

```
Look at how I deliver this kind of project, from my notes above. Suggest 4 to 6 stage names a client would understand with no explanation. One or 2 words each. Then show them as one line with the current stage marked, like this: Kickoff > Draft > [Review] > Final > Launch.
```

**Step 5. Put it where they can open it.** Paste the page into a doc. Share it view-only. Check the link on your phone, signed out, so you see what they see.

## Part 3. Keep it alive in a few minutes

The page changes when the work moves. Not on a schedule.

**Step 6. Update from your work notes.** Paste the current page and a line or 2 about what changed.

```
Here's my client's current progress page, and below it what changed today. Update the page. Move finished things to Done so far. Rewrite Where we are in one sentence. Refresh What's next and What I need from you. Change the date at the top to today. Keep the same 5 blocks and the same plain voice. Then give me a 2-sentence message I can send the client that says what changed and links them to the page.
```

**Step 7. Check before you share.** Read it once. Check every date and every "done". Only mark something done if you've looked at the finished thing yourself.

## Part 4. Train the client to look first

**Step 8. Put the link everywhere.** One line under your name in every email to that client: "Your progress page: [link]".

**Step 9. Answer with the page.** The next time they ask where things are, update the page first, then reply with one line and the link.

## The one thing to remember

Clients don't chase you because they're worried about the work. They chase you because they can't see it.

## You're done when

- One client has a page with the 5 blocks.
- The stages are named in plain words and the current one is marked.
- You opened the link on your phone, signed out, and it worked.
- You updated the page once using the update prompt.
- The link is in your email sign-off for that client.

## What this doesn't do

- It's a shared doc, not a portal. No login, no brand, and you make each client's page by hand.
- It doesn't update itself.
- It doesn't handle onboarding, payments, guides or messages.
- Past a handful of clients, updating pages one by one becomes its own job.
